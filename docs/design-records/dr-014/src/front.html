About this record

This is a design record: the reasoning, geometry, motion, material and defect history behind a single control. It documents the RMH hub — the orb docked at the bottom centre of every standard page on rmhstudios.com, and the radial dial that blooms out of it.

What it is drawn from

Everything here is reconstructed from the repository, which is the platform's own stated source of truth for conflicting information. The primary sources are components/radial/RadialHub.tsx ({{stat:hubLines}} lines), components/radial/radial.css ({{stat:cssLines}} lines), components/radial/README.md, lib/sidebar-nav.ts, docs/design-language.md, docs/ui-audit-2026-07-28.md, and the fifteen commits between e5003b1 and feeec2c that built, broke and repaired the control.

How the figures were made

Every dial diagram in this book is drawn by the production geometry, not by hand. The figure generator carries a faithful port of the component's RINGS, SINGLE_RING, INNER_MAX, SINGLE_RING_MAX, ART_BLEED_DEG and ringSector(), and renders the same annulus sectors the browser clips. A figure therefore cannot quietly disagree with the code it illustrates: change the geometry and the figures change with it.

Code listings are extracted from the working tree at build time, with their real file paths and line numbers. Nothing in a listing is retyped.

A note on the live site

The live production site was not reachable from the environment this record was compiled in — outbound requests to rmhstudios.com were refused by the network policy at the proxy, not by the site. Every observation about rendered behaviour therefore comes from the code, from the shipped CSS, and from the July 2026 visual audit, which captured the interface across thirteen viewports and three themes and is quoted throughout with its finding identifiers intact.

Reading order

Parts I and II are the argument: what a home button is for, what the site had before, and why a radial dial was the answer rather than a hamburger. Parts III to V are the build: geometry, motion, material. Part VI is input and accessibility. Part VII is the defect log — the most useful part for anyone maintaining the thing. Part VIII is verification and open questions. The appendices are reference: annotated source, a full geometry table, the token contract, the commit index and the audit index.

Conventions

Ring radii are always given as a percentage of the dial's width, matching the component, because that is the unit clip-path resolves against. The dial is square, so its radius is 50% and the rim sits at r = 50. Angles run clockwise from twelve o'clock, which in CSS coordinates is −90°.
