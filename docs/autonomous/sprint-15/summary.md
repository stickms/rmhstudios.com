# Sprint 15 — summary

Twenty features shipped on `claude/autonomous-sprint-15`. Each was built from its brief in its own worktree, reviewed by a fresh reviewer who fixed what they found, and merged wave by wave (plan: [plan.md](plan.md)).

## What shipped
- **01 Human Keyboard.** The last RMHBox stub whose every input is a discrete key press is a playable cooperative minigame. The 26 letters are dealt among 3 to 10 players and reshuffled on a timer with a warning, and the table types one of 208 bundled sentences together, each player pressing only the letters they hold. The hub judges every press: the holder advances the sentence, anyone else hears "Not yours", and a wrong held letter locks the table for 500 ms. Spaces advance on their own, presses are limited to five a second per player, and a dropped player's letters go to the others after 5 s so the sentence never stalls. A player's state carries only their own letters; the table sees counts and spectators see every deal. Scoring and the team multiplier follow the design spec once per round, with a settings schema (1 to 3 rounds, a 6 to 15 s reshuffle), history detail and a match log. At rest nothing runs: one `setTimeout` a second, only while typing. Review made the lock's end fire the countdown's one timeout when that is sooner, moved the sentence's reading into visually hidden text (an `aria-label` on a paragraph was never read), added the Not My Job award and the named award icons, logged reshuffles and re-deals, and made the history searchable by sentence.
- **02 Your House Always Wins game, and your Nightrail lines, on every device.** Both games join the shared save table with the local copy still written first and its keys unchanged. The kit's transport is last-writer-wins and treats a 409 as a failure, so a new compare-and-set transport (`lib/game-saves/cas-transport.ts`, the Hearthworks model) sends the `updatedAt` it last read or wrote (null for no row); a 409 stops uploads until the next read, and the `pagehide` beacon carries the same stamp. The House's run is three keys, so `lib/house-always-wins/cloud.ts` composes and splits `{main, ngplus, meta}` itself: a corrupt key is never uploaded, an unreadable account copy is never written over, and a chosen copy is written back only after the whole payload parsed. The menu says where the run is saved; conflicts open the shared dialog; Nightrail merges as a union of opened lines and never asks. Review gave the run a reset count, so "New run" cannot be undone by a second device holding the older, further run; stamped a signed-out run as the guest's; wrote the New Game+ record back with the chosen copy; made the next menu visit after a 409 (or a beacon on a restored page) read the account again; and made the transport send one write at a time.
- **03 Slice It in the developer API.** The two Slice It routes the API already served but `ENDPOINTS` never listed (the charts list and the leaderboard) are catalogued in a Slice It group, and a new guard fails any v1 route file without an entry and any entry without a file. Three reads are new: a chart's details (`GET /slice-it/charts/{id}`), a player's public stats by handle (`GET /slice-it/players/{handle}`; hidden, banned, handle-less or blocked either way is one 404, and no member id is sent) and the key owner's runs (`GET /me/slice-it/runs`, keyset paged, the `BigInt` id as a string, under the new explicit `read:slice-it-runs` scope). The module is in `BOARD_PRIVACY_READ_PATHS`. Review made every read `private, max-age=30` rather than the spec's `public` (each call is key-gated and quota-counted, and a player's answer depends on the key owner's blocks), typed the nullable leaderboard and career fields in the SDK, had the player read check the member's own `hideFromLeaderboards` (the shared hidden set is capped), and made the registry guard match any indent.
- **04 Report a TF2 HUD, a TF2 loadout or a Versecraft story, and take your own story down.** Three report types (`tf2-hud`, `tf2-loadout`, `versecraft-seed`) follow the member-item path decks and rooms use, with queue previews and Your reports rows. "Resolve and remove" stamps a new `moderatedAt`, and a HUD or loadout is made `PRIVATE` in the same update, so a later restore never republishes it. `WORKSHOP_READ_PATHS` lists every reader with a source scan: a removed item is refused to everyone but its owner, who sees it marked and cannot publish, rename, copy or like it. The three public lists leave out blocked and banned authors, and `DELETE /api/versecraft/seeds/:id` lets an author take a story down. Review filed automated screening's story flags under the new report type (the old type had no takedown branch), refused ratings across a block, and widened the source scan to any client name, relation reads and raw SQL on the three tables.
- **05 Prediction comments that respect blocks, and that you can delete or report.** `listComments` leaves out the viewer's blocked (either way) and muted authors and banned authors for everyone, and pages 20 at a time on `(createdAt, id)`. `DELETE /api/predictions/comments/$id` removes a comment for its author or an admin (audited in the same transaction); anyone else gets the same 404 as an unknown id. `prediction-comment` is reportable and removable (deleted), with a market-and-side queue preview. MarketPanel gets a per-comment menu and "Show earlier comments", and the member's comments join the export. Review added handler-level route tests, made the Your reports fake apply the market-visibility filter, moved the menu's strings out from under an aliased `t` that `i18n:extract` would have filed in the wrong namespace, faded in appended rows (M9), and wrapped the leaderboard names `ForecastingPanel` showed.
- **06 Report a guestbook entry or an announcement comment.** Two removable member-item types, `guestbook-entry` (deleted) and `blog-comment` (soft-deleted), eligible only when the reporter can read the text where it is shown; a guestbook's owner may report an entry in their own book. The announcement discussion's list and its total leave out authors the viewer blocked, was blocked by or muted, and banned authors, through one shared where-builder. The guestbook's two refusals are coded (`GUESTBOOK_CLOSED`, `GUESTBOOK_DAILY_LIMIT`), report buttons sit in each entry's and comment's actions, and both join the export. Review pinned the whole announcement filter in the test (it had been loosened to `objectContaining`) and held that the count uses the same where as the list.
- **07 Hear back when a puzzle you reported is reviewed, and when a jam moves its deadlines.** An admin marks a mode-day's open puzzle reports reviewed from `/admin/daily-puzzles` with an outcome (fault or stands) and an optional note. Each report is claimed by a conditional update on `reviewedAt`, so a second review changes and sends nothing, and each claimed reporter gets one notice after the commit; the review never touches the puzzle or the scores, and no notice carries an answer. A report on an already reviewed day is stored reviewed and the dialog shows the outcome. A jam save that moves a deadline after the start tells entrants and accepted teammates (not the saver, at most 500) once; a description-only edit tells nobody. Review skipped banned and pending-deletion members before the cap, logged a capped save, loaded the namespaces the inbox borrows so the outcome rendered translated, and gave the review note a visible label.
- **08 Keep your RMHCalculator graphs, and send one as a link.** Members save up to 50 graphs (title, functions, domain and the server-validated result), open one from "Saved graphs" with no model call, rename or delete it, and copy a link that fills the same functions and domain into anyone's calculator; the link carries no points, is parsed with limits and never plots by itself. The cap is checked under a per-member advisory lock. A stored result that no longer parses opens as "Plot again", and plotting again updates the row only when the functions and domain are unchanged. The Notebook tab's four untyped calls move onto contracts and React Query. Graphs and notebooks join the export; `CalcGraph` is erased. Review rounded points to six significant digits before a save (the largest result the schema allows was about 66 KB, past the routes' 64 KB body limit), refused C1 controls in links, and replaced the folder-wide user-text exemption with the three files that show member text.
- **09 Dream Rift co-op runs the hub vouches for.** The hub records the run when a co-op game starts, with the lobby's accounts by slot from the socket session (a slow insert never holds the start), and sends its id to the room. Only the host can finish it, once, within six hours, through one conditional update; the body names no account. The board lists finished hub runs and rows from before the feature, resolves every slot at read time, and shows a member who turned boards off or is banned as "A player"; "Your co-op runs" lists a member's own runs 20 a page. A nightly job deletes unfinished runs over a day old. Erasure removes the member from every run, new and old, and the export carries their runs. Three columns, a GIN index on `memberIds` (`CONCURRENTLY`, with its `_ensure_valid` repair) and `COOP_RUN_UNKNOWN`. Review required the post to cover exactly the recorded seats and every member's seat (a host could drop a member and invent a guest), and moved the `memberIds` replacement into the erasure transaction.
- **10 Notifications in the developer API that you can mark read.** Each item of `GET /api/v1/me/notifications` gains `key` and `params` (the site notice's translation key and values; null for a plain notification). The cursor is `<createdAt>|<id>` with a tuple comparison (the old bare-time cursor is still read), and `unread=true` filters. `GET …/unread-count` answers the site badge's own number, and `POST …/read` marks the key owner's ids (1 to 100) or everything unread at or before a time through the inbox's own `setRead` and `markAllRead` (which gains `before`), under the new explicit `write:notifications` scope with `Idempotency-Key`. All changes are additive and in the changelog. Review dropped params that are not strings or finite numbers and pinned the list's existing shape.
- **11 Track class resources, and take a short rest that spends Hit Dice.** Counted class resources (Rages, Channel Divinity, Second Wind…) are tracked by name on the character, and each rest gives back what the class data's recharge text says (parsed once in `engine/dnd5e/rest.ts`). Short Rest opens a dialog that spends Hit Dice per die size and rolls them with the Constitution modifier (at least 1 a die), logs them and, when rolling for the table, shares them with the party. A shared sheet shows what is left read-only, and old saves load full. Review recorded which die sizes were spent (`hitDiceSpent`), so a multiclass sheet gives back the right dice and a long rest returns the largest first; tagged a table roll "Hit Die" with a fixed literal; kept dice already posted when the table refuses a later one; and listed a resource two classes share once.
- **12 Mention someone in a game discussion.** A new thread or reply tells each member it names (the first five unique handles, never the writer) with a MENTION notice that opens the post. Blocks either way, mutes, bans, pending deletions and the thread's own mute are honoured, and a member who would also get the reply notice gets the mention only. The thread page links known handles from one lookup (an unknown handle stays text, nothing is stored), and both composers get the feed's `@` autocomplete in a new `atOnly` mode. Review made the cap one unread mention per member per thread whoever wrote it (`dedupeAnyActor`), and counted a member as told only when the notice was really delivered (`deliverNotification`), so a member with mention notices off still gets the reply notice.
- **13 Suggest a theme for a game jam, and delete a jam that has not started.** Members suggest up to three themes for a theme-voted jam before the vote opens, seen only by themselves and admins; the comment timeout, bans and blocks by the creator apply, and the count and insert run under the member's lock. Admins see the suggestions grouped by text in the shortlist dialog and add one in a click. "On the shortlist" is shown to a suggester, and exported, only once the vote has opened, so the shortlist and the secret theme do not leak. Admins delete a jam before its start with no entries in one conditional `deleteMany` under the jam's lock (which the entry write now takes too), with an audit row. Suggestions are erased and exported.
- **14 Your tournaments, and what each place won.** `GET /api/tournaments/mine` (private, no-store) lists the tournaments a member entered or hosted, newest first, keyset paged on `(createdAt, id)`; a cancelled tournament the member only entered is left out. Compete > Tournaments gains a "Yours" tab with the place ("3rd of 16" or "Host") and the prize. A finished tournament's page shows each entrant's prize, the pool and the rake, and a refund line when cancelled. Read-only by test: nothing writes payouts, placements or coins. The export lists entries, hosted tournaments and payouts. Review added `tournament(createdById, createdAt)` and `tournament_entrant(userId)` (each `CONCURRENTLY` with its repair; both halves seq-scanned on a 50k-row copy) and fell back to Open when a viewer who had "Yours" selected signed out.
- **15 Your head-to-head record in Synapse Storm.** "Your matches" has a Matches / Rivals switch. Rivals lists each named account the member finished versus matches with (matches together, wins, losses, ties, the latest match) from one raw self-join of `ss_player_match` cut to 50; only matches both sides finished count, and guests, erased members, blocks either way, bans and members hidden from the boards are never rivals. A rival row carries the rival's own match-row id as an opaque key, never an account id, and `with=<key>` on the matches read narrows the same keyset-paged list to the pair's matches. Review made the heading one translatable "Matches with {{name}}" line with the name isolated. Integration listed the reader in `BOARD_PRIVACY_READ_PATHS` at merge.
- **16 Hear when a report takes something of yours down, and ask for a second look.** `decideReports` records each takedown of a member's item (`ContentRemoval`, unique on the report, no reporter stored) and sends one keyed notice, the message variant replacing `report-decision`. A reversible removal can be contested once within 14 days from a "Removed after a report" list in the Account status section of `/settings/security` (no new page; the legacy redirect stays); an admin upholds or restores it from a Removal reviews tab on `/admin/appeals`. A restore clears `moderatedAt` only and never publishes. The author never sees the report id, reason, count, reporter or moderator. Review split the decided notice into one key per outcome (the stored English and the preview disagreed), kept a typed moderator message when the removal notice fails, and said "You can publish it again" only for kinds whose takedown turned the owner's switch off.
- **17 Community packs for Identity Crisis and Human Keyboard, and a trusted auto-hide.** A host plays a member's own identities (with aliases after a bar) or sentences by pack code, as Wit War packs already work. One pure module (`lib/party/pack-games.ts`) says what a line is for each game and serves the create schema, the packs page's preview (unusable lines with their line numbers before saving) and the hub's loader, which re-reads every stored line and falls back to the bundled content on any failure. The auto-hide now counts a reporter only when the account is at least 7 days old, verified or linked, and not banned, decided in one query under the report lock; other reports still reach the queue, which shows "Counted toward the auto-hide: N of M" only. Making a pack honours a reply timeout. Review stopped an e-mail and password sign-in (`providerId 'credential'`) counting as a linked provider, guarded both handlers' snapshots while a pack is loading, stripped control and bidirectional characters from lines, bounded the guess matcher's cache, and fixed the Identity Crisis settings product test.
- **18 Close the gaps sprint 13 left open.** S13-06: erasure publishes the member id after the commit and the RMHTube hub renames the member in every running room (chat, pin, reply quotes, presence, queue credits, ban list); review matched the client reducer to the hub and logged a missing Redis. S13-09: an all-day entry lands on its own day in every zone, and review regrouped timed entries by the viewer's zone with a UTC first render, so the calendar hydrates. S13-12: the unused reading key is gone. S13-15: a rider can report a trip cancelled after a driver accepted it. S13-17: two concurrent message edits keep both earlier versions (`FOR NO KEY UPDATE`, after review, so reactions and thread replies do not wait). S13-19: hidden party members carry `nameHidden` in the developer API.
- **19 Fix what sprint 14's integration found.** A daily score stored with no result no longer crashes Alibi, Chainlink, Outcast or Spectrum: each reads its result through `lib/daily-puzzles/result-shapes.ts`, and a score with nothing to replay opens "Played on another device" (score and time only). A new `Handle` primitive (`components/ui/handle.tsx`, a `<bdi>`) keeps the `@` first in right-to-left pages; every printed handle outside the Velum 2099 game moved onto it, and a ratchet holds the rest at 3. The conversation header's chat settings are the controls row's last item at every width, so focus order matches the eye. Review widened the ratchet to multi-line and template-literal prints and converted them.
- **20 Close the gaps sprint 14 left open.** An Identity Crisis finalist reads `final` to everyone but themselves and spectators until REVEAL, and a final guess's points join the public scores only then; a new suite deals from a community pack and checks no wire shows it early. A self-exclusion reaches every open tab through a `BroadcastChannel` (one reference-counted listener, no timer or poll). `rmhbox_match.winnerUserId` is indexed (`CONCURRENTLY`, with its repair), and erasure clears signed-out security reports sent from the member's address (case-insensitive). A co-op player outside Altair's top ten sees their own place ("You: 34th") from a per-caller route; review declared that route private and registered it with board privacy.
